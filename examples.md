# Examples

Three worked cases. They show the reasoning, not just the conclusion.

The cases are constructed for teaching, with reports written to make each agreement pattern
legible. They are not real reports and the papers do not exist. The third one finds no defect,
which is the outcome an author is least likely to reach alone and the one that saves the most
time.

Between them they show the three agreement patterns — convergent, divergent, and no pattern at
all — and two of the three verdicts on a prior revision: one qualifying revision that returned a
positive result at the stage it acted on, and one case with no prior round to read.

**None of these cases assesses whether a reviewer was right.** That question is not answerable
from three reports and is not what the folder does.

---

## Case 1 — Break at Stage 4

**Input:** Methods paper, second submission to the same venue after a reject-with-invitation.
Three independent reports on the current round, three on the prior round, both decision
letters, the submitted version, the revision summary. Venue publishes a 78% rejection rate,
publishes that it does not run a reviewer discussion phase, and declares reviewer areas.

### Failure observed

Rejected. The venue rejects 78% of submissions, so the decision alone is weak evidence — but
all three reports independently raise the same substantive objection, and the decision letter
quotes it. That is a convergent panel and it carries signal. Failure confirmed.

### Comparison set

Three independent reports on the current round. The venue publishes that reviewers do not see
each other's reports and that resubmissions are routed to a fresh panel, and the reviewer
numbering confirms no overlap with the prior round. Declared areas differ across all three.
Prior round: three reports, same venue, same editor, same scope statement.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable.** Three independent, substantive, complete reports on one version. All three quote
the paper and name specific sections. Declared areas span three different subfields, which
matters here: convergence from readers with shared training would be biased toward agreement,
and this panel does not have shared training. No single report carries the pattern — removing
any one leaves the objection present in the other two.

The confidential-to-editor sections were supplied for one report of three.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Reading | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Desk survival | Passed | The paper went to a full panel |
| 2. Reviewer engagement | Healthy | All three quote the claim and name sections |
| 3. Reconstruction | Healthy | All three restatements agree with each other and with the paper's stated claim |
| 4. Claim support | **Convergent objection** | All three independently name the same gap |
| 5. Contribution judgment | Starved | Two reports do not reach it; the third's assessment is conditional on the Stage 4 objection |

Pass-through, reconstruction to claim support: **three of the three reviewers who reconstructed
the paper correctly raised the objection.** That is the figure that matters, and it is not the
same as "three reviewers objected."

### Primary constraint

**Stage 4, claim support.** Readers arrived at the paper — their restatements agree with each
other and with what the paper says it claims — and did not accept that the evidence supports it.

Stages 1 through 3 are not the diagnosis site, and no conclusion is drawn about the paper's
clarity, framing, or structure. Those demonstrably worked this round. Stage 5 is starved: two
reports never reach a contribution judgment, and the third makes one explicitly conditional on
the Stage 4 objection being resolved, so it carries no independent information about whether the
paper clears this venue's bar.

### Primary cause

**Missing comparison.** The evaluation cannot separate the contribution from an assumption it
shares with its only baseline.

### Mechanism

The paper's central claim is that the proposed estimator improves on existing practice. It is
evaluated against one baseline, and that baseline shares the paper's key modelling assumption.
All three reports arrive at the same consequence independently: any improvement observed could
be produced by the shared assumption rather than by the estimator, so the comparison as
constructed cannot attribute the gain to the contribution. Two reports name the same specific
alternative baseline that would break the tie; the third describes the same comparison without
naming a candidate.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

Convergence is the discriminating evidence, and its strength rests on the panel being
independent and heterogeneous. The venue publishes that reviewers do not see each other's
reports. Declared areas differ across all three. Three readers with different training, working
alone, reached the same objection about the same comparison — which is what a property of the
work looks like, and is not what a property of any one reader looks like.

The pass-through is what makes this a Stage 4 finding rather than a Stage 3 one. All three
restatements of the claim agree with each other and with the paper, so none of these objections
is downstream of a misreading. Had one reviewer restated the claim differently and objected from
there, that objection would belong to Stage 3 and would carry no independent weight here.

The recommendations split — one major revision, two rejects — and that split is not read as
polarization. All three describe the same defect. They differ on whether it is fatal, which is a
statement about their thresholds rather than about the paper.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Claim ambiguity (Stage 3).** Not supported, and the prior round is readable here. The
first-round panel was divergent: three reports, three mutually incompatible restatements of the
contribution. The author revised for clarity — rewrote the abstract, added an explicit
claim-scope statement, restructured the method section — and changed nothing else: no new
experiments, no new data, no reframing of the literature. The resubmission went to the same
venue under the same editor and the same scope statement, and the venue routed it to a fresh
independent panel.

**That is a qualifying revision, and it tested Stage 3.** It worked. All three current
restatements agree with each other and with the paper, where none did before. **Positive at
Stage 3**, and the rejection persisted — because a second constraint sat underneath the first
and was invisible while readers were still arriving at different papers. Two findings, not one.
Clearing a break can reveal rather than resolve.

**Framing mismatch (Stage 3).** Not supported. The restatements agree with the paper, not merely
with each other.

**Venue scope or incrementality (Stage 5).** Not supported and starved. No report disputes fit,
and the one contribution judgment on record is conditional on the Stage 4 objection.

**Reviewer mismatch (Stage 2).** Not supported. All three demonstrate command of the area:
each quotes the claim, names sections, and one cites the relevant prior baseline by name.

**"A reviewer did not read it."** Not supported and directly contradicted. All three
reconstructions are accurate against the submitted version.

### Null model

Rejected. The venue's 78% rate makes rejection ordinary, and an ordinary rejection would show
scattered minor objections and a letter citing competition. This letter quotes a specific
objection, and that objection appears independently in all three reports. A panel that converges
on one substantive gap is not triage.

### Confidence

**Supported.** The panel is Usable, the funnel locates the constraint, the pass-through confirms
the objections come from accurate readings, and the null was tested against the venue's
published rate and rejected.

### Missing evidence

Confidential-to-editor sections for two of three reports. Those sections typically carry fit and
novelty commentary, so their absence could only add Stage 5 signal — and Stage 5 sits downstream
of a located Stage 4 break, where it is starved and uninformative either way. **The gap therefore
cannot move the constraint**, and the conclusion survives the worst case in which both withheld
sections are full of contribution objections.

### What would prove this wrong

A restatement in any of the three reports that conflicts with the paper's stated claim, which
would move that report's objection to Stage 3 and break the pass-through this finding rests on.
Or evidence that the panel was not independent after all — a discussion phase the published
process does not mention — which would make the convergence uninterpretable.

## Case 2 — Break at Stage 3

**Input:** Empirical paper, first submission. Three reports, decision letter, submitted version.
Venue's rejection rate published at 71%. The venue does not publish whether it runs a reviewer
discussion phase.

### Failure observed

Rejected. Three substantive reports, and no substantive objection appears in more than one of
them. Each report's account of what the paper claims differs from the other two. That is a
divergent panel, and divergence is a finding rather than noise. Failure confirmed.

### Comparison set

Three reports, all author-facing sections supplied in full, all on the single submitted version.
No prior submission. No confidential-to-editor sections supplied. Reviewer areas not declared by
this venue.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable with limitations.** Three substantive reports on one version, all quoting the paper and
naming sections, and the author confirms all reports received are present. The limitation is
independence: this venue does not publish its process, so whether reviewers saw each other's
reports before finalising could not be established.

**Which way it pushes.** A discussion phase biases a panel *toward* agreement. The finding here
is divergence — three readings that do not converge — so if discussion did occur, these readers
failed to converge despite an opportunity to, and the unclarity is worse than the reports alone
suggest. **The limitation pushes against the finding and therefore strengthens it.** The
conclusion survives the worst case.

It does not lift the cap. Confidence caps at Provisional on structure, not on the direction of
the bias.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Reading | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Desk survival | Passed | Full panel |
| 2. Reviewer engagement | Eliminated structurally | See below |
| 3. Reconstruction | **Restatements conflict** | Three mutually incompatible accounts of the contribution |
| 4. Claim support | Starved | Every objection is downstream of a different reading |
| 5. Contribution judgment | Starved | Two novelty assessments, each about a different paper |

**Stage 2 is eliminated structurally rather than comparatively.** There is no baseline for report
length at this venue and none was supplied, so engagement cannot be tested by comparison. It does
not need to be: a disengaged reviewer cannot produce a detailed restatement of a paper's method,
and all three reports produce one. A break at Stage 2 would starve Stage 3, and Stage 3 is
populated. Recorded as structural.

### Primary constraint

**Stage 3, reconstruction.** Three qualified readers arrived at three different papers.

Stage 4 and Stage 5 are starved and no conclusion is drawn from them. This matters more here
than the phrasing suggests: the reports contain eleven substantive objections between them, some
of them specific and forceful, and **none of them is evidence about whether the paper's evidence
supports its claims**, because each was written about a differently reconstructed claim.

### Primary cause

**Claim ambiguity.** The paper asserts at two scopes and never marks which is being claimed.

### Mechanism

The abstract states the contribution twice: once as a general property of the approach, once as
a result obtained on the study's single population. Neither statement is qualified against the
other, and the discussion section restates both without reconciling them. Reviewer 1 reads the
general claim and asks for additional populations, which the paper would need if that is the
claim. Reviewer 2 reads the narrow claim and asks why a single-population result warrants the
framing, which is the right question if that is the claim. Reviewer 3 reads it as a position
paper and asks for engagement with a literature the paper never invokes. Each objection is
internally correct. They are answers to three different papers, and the paper contains the
material for all three readings.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

The restatements are the evidence, and they were extracted before any objection was read.
Reviewer 1's account and Reviewer 2's account are mutually exclusive: one describes a claim about
the approach in general, the other a claim about this population. Both cannot be what the paper
says. Reviewer 3's account is incompatible with both.

Each report is internally coherent. That is the signature of ambiguity rather than of
inattention: a reviewer who skimmed produces vague objections, and these are specific, sourced,
and correct given each reviewer's premise.

The objections follow from the restatements. Reviewer 1's request for more populations is exactly
what the general reading requires; it is not an independent objection that happens to coexist with
a misreading.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Framing mismatch (Stage 3).** Not supported, and this is the discriminator that matters at this
stage. Framing mismatch and claim ambiguity both produce reports whose account of the paper
differs from the author's, and both produce objections that miss — the count of misreadings is
identical and separates nothing. What separates them is **agreement among the readers**. Under
framing mismatch the restatements agree with each other and differ only from the author's: the
paper communicated one thing to everyone, and it was the wrong thing. Here the restatements
conflict with each other. Three readers, three papers.

**An evidential defect (Stage 4).** Not supported and starved. Eleven substantive objections are
on record and not one of them is usable, because each was written about a different claim. This is
the demotion an author will find hardest and it is the point of the folder.

**Reviewer mismatch (Stage 2).** Not supported. Each report demonstrates command of the area it
believes the paper belongs to. The problem is that they believe three different things.

**"Reviewer 2 did not read it."** Not supported. Reviewer 2's restatement is a defensible reading
of the submitted abstract. So are the other two, which is the finding.

**Prior revision.** None. This is a first submission, so there is no completed experiment to read,
which is worth stating rather than leaving as an absence.

### Null model

Rejected. The venue's 71% rate makes rejection ordinary, and an ordinary rejection shows scattered
minor objections against an agreed account of the paper. Here the accounts themselves disagree.
Divergence at the level of what the paper *is* is not triage, and it is not something a
high rejection rate explains.

### Confidence

**Provisional.** The constraint is located and the mechanism is well supported, but the panel is
Usable with limitations — independence could not be established — and that caps confidence
regardless of the direction the limitation pushes.

### Missing evidence

The venue's review process, specifically whether a discussion phase occurred. Confidential-to-editor
sections, which sometimes state a reviewer's reading more plainly than the author-facing text.
Reviewer areas, undeclared by this venue: if all three readers came from the same subfield, the
divergence is more striking still, and if they came from three, less so — but not less than
divergent, since the restatements conflict on the paper's own terms rather than on disciplinary
ones.

### What would prove this wrong

Restatements that agree with each other once read closely — if the three accounts turn out to be
the same reading in different vocabulary, this becomes a framing finding rather than an ambiguity
one, and the mechanism changes. Or a claim-scope statement in the submitted version that the
analysis missed, which would move the finding to Stage 3D, structural burial, and relocate the
mechanism from ambiguity to placement.

## Case 3 — Null

**Input:** Three reports, decision letter, submitted version. Venue publishes an 85% rejection
rate and publishes that it runs a reviewer discussion phase before final recommendations. The
author is preparing a substantial revision and wants to know what to fix.

### Failure observed

Rejected at a venue that rejects 85% of what it receives. No substantive objection appears in
more than one report. The objections present are requests for additional related work, a clearer
figure, and a longer limitations paragraph. The decision letter cites the volume of submissions
and the size of the accepted programme, and names no defect.

On the venue's published rate, rejection is the modal outcome, and this decision carries no
information about this paper. **No failure demonstrated.**

### Comparison set

Three reports, all sections supplied, all on the single submitted version. Venue's published
rejection rate: 85%. Venue's published process: reviewers see each other's reports and may revise
before final recommendation.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable with limitations.** Three substantive reports on one version, complete, all quoting the
paper. The limitation is independence, and here it is not a gap in the record but a published
feature of the process: this venue runs a discussion phase, so the reports were finalised after
the reviewers saw each other.

**Which way it pushes.** A discussion phase biases *toward* agreement. The finding here rests on
the absence of agreement — no substantive objection appears twice. If these readers had an
opportunity to converge and did not, the absence of a shared objection is more meaningful than it
would be on an independent panel. **The limitation pushes against the finding and therefore
strengthens it.**

The cap still binds. Confidence caps at Provisional on the structure of the panel, not on the
direction of its bias. Those are separate questions and the first does not answer the second.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Reading | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Desk survival | Passed | Full panel |
| 2. Reviewer engagement | Healthy | All three quote the paper and name sections |
| 3. Reconstruction | Healthy | All three restatements agree with each other and with the paper |
| 4. Claim support | No convergent objection | Eight minor objections, no overlap |
| 5. Contribution judgment | No dispute | Two reports call the contribution appropriate for the venue; none disputes fit |

No stage reads below par.

### Primary constraint

**None located.** The panel reconstructs the paper correctly, raises nothing it agrees on, and
does not dispute the paper's fit.

### Primary cause

**No paper-specific cause is demonstrated.**

### Mechanism

The venue rejects 85% of submissions, which means the ordinary outcome for a competent paper here
is rejection. This panel produced the profile that accompanies that outcome: accurate
reconstructions, no convergent substantive objection, no scope dispute, and a decision letter
citing programme size. Eight minor objections across three reports is the ordinary furniture of
review and would appear on an accepted paper at the same venue. The decision was made in a queue,
not about a defect.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

A paper with a locatable constraint fails at an identifiable stage. This one does not. Readers
arrived at it, agreed with each other about what it is, raised nothing in common against it, and
did not question whether it belongs. Every stage that can be read is at par.

The absence of convergence is the load-bearing observation, and it is stronger here than on a
normal panel because these reviewers saw each other's reports before finalising and still did not
land on a shared objection.

The eight objections are individually reasonable and collectively uninformative. Requests for more
related work and a clearer figure appear on most reports at most venues, including on accepted
papers, and no diagnosis can be built on them.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**An evidential defect (Stage 4).** Not supported. No objection to the evidence appears in more
than one report, and a real defect visible to one qualified reader is usually visible to another.

**Claim ambiguity or framing (Stage 3).** Not supported. The three restatements agree with each
other and with the paper.

**Venue scope mismatch (Stage 5).** Not supported. No report disputes fit and two affirm it.

**Competitive triage (5D).** Considered, and it is the closest live alternative to the null rather
than a rival to it. 5D describes a paper that cleared the bar and lost a queue; the null describes
a decision carrying no signal. The two reports affirming the contribution's fit are weak evidence
for 5D. They are not enough to call it, because neither ranks the paper against the accepted
programme and no such ranking was supplied. Named, not chosen.

**Prior revision.** None. First submission, so there is no completed experiment to read.

### Null model

**Accepted. This is the diagnosis.** The venue's published rate makes rejection ordinary, the
panel raises nothing it agrees on, reconstruction is clean, and fit is not disputed.

### Confidence

**Provisional**, under the null-grading rung in `rules.md`.

The middle clause of that rung is satisfied: the null was tested against the input built to test
it — the venue's published 85% rate — rather than inferred from an impression that the reports
seemed mild. What fails is the first clause. The panel is Usable with limitations rather than
Usable, because the discussion phase compromises independence, and that caps the grade even
though the direction of the bias strengthens the finding.

Supported was reachable here and is not reached, and the reason is structural.

### Missing evidence

Where this paper sat against the accepted programme, which is the input that would separate the
null from competitive triage. Reviewer areas, undeclared: a panel drawn from one subfield could
share a blind spot, and **this comparison set is structurally unable to detect that**, because
the perspective does not vary across it. A reading from outside the panel's shared background is
what would test it. That alternative is named rather than ruled out.

### What would prove this wrong

A convergent substantive objection recoverable from the confidential-to-editor sections, which
were supplied here but are brief. Or a venue rejection rate materially lower than the published
figure for this track, which would make an ordinary rejection less ordinary and return the
decision to signal-carrying.

---

**Why this case is in the set.**
The author asked what to fix. The evidence does not identify anything. No stage reads below par,
the panel agrees on nothing against the paper, and the decision letter names no defect.

Naming that is the diagnosis. A revision aimed at eight scattered minor comments will produce a
different paper with the same standing, because the standing was not about the paper. What to do
with a competent paper at a venue that rejects 85% of them is the author's decision, and this
folder does not have an opinion about it.
