*International Conference on Interaction Systems.* Published rejection rate: **85%**.

Scope statement: "systems, studies, and methods across interaction research."

## The submitted paper

**Title:** "Recovery paths after interruption in multi-step form completion"

**Abstract, in full:**

> Interruptions during multi-step form completion are common and their costs are poorly
> characterised. We report a controlled study (n = 312) measuring completion time and error rate
> following interruptions at three points in a five-step flow. Recovery cost is not uniform:
> interruption at step 2 costs 2.1× the recovery time of interruption at step 4, and error rates
> at resumption rise with the number of fields already committed. We describe the pattern and its
> implications for form design.

**Structure:** 1. Introduction · 2. Related work · 3. Method · 4. Results · 5. Discussion ·
6. Limitations

## Reviewer 1

> **Summary.** A controlled study of interruption recovery in multi-step form completion, n=312,
> interruptions at three points in a five-step flow. Finds recovery cost varies by interruption
> point, with step 2 costing about twice step 4, and error rates rising with committed fields.
>
> **Assessment.** Competently done. The design is appropriate to the question, the analysis is
> straightforward and correctly reported, and the limitations section is honest about the
> single-flow constraint. I did not find anything wrong with it.
>
> The related work could engage more with the task-switching literature, which has treated
> resumption cost in adjacent settings.
>
> **Recommendation:** Weak accept.

## Reviewer 2

> **Summary.** The authors measure recovery cost after interruptions at three points in a
> five-step form, reporting that earlier interruptions cost more to recover from and that error
> rates scale with committed fields.
>
> **Assessment.** This is solid work. Method is clear, the effect is reported with appropriate
> caution, and the discussion does not overreach. I have no substantive objection.
>
> Figure 3 is hard to read at print size — the three conditions are distinguished by shade rather
> than by marker.
>
> **Recommendation:** Weak accept.

## Reviewer 3

> **Summary.** A study of interruption recovery in a five-step form flow, n=312, measuring
> completion time and errors following interruption at steps 2, 3 and 4.
>
> **Assessment.** Well-executed and clearly written. The finding that recovery cost is
> non-uniform across interruption points is a sensible contribution to this venue and I would be
> content to see it in the programme.
>
> Minor: the limitations section could say more about whether the effect would hold for flows
> longer than five steps.
>
> **Recommendation:** Weak accept.

## Objections raised, in full

Across the three reports:

1. Related work could engage the task-switching literature (R1)
2. Figure 3 unreadable at print size (R2)
3. Limitations could address longer flows (R3)

**No objection appears in more than one report.** No report disputes the paper's fit, novelty, or
correctness. Two reports state the contribution is appropriate for the venue.
