# Failure modes

Organized by funnel stage, because the stage where the submission breaks determines which
causes are even eligible. Each entry gives the signature that distinguishes it, and the
counter-signature that rules it out.

---

Canonical funnel, used throughout: **1. Desk survival · 2. Reviewer engagement ·
3. Reconstruction · 4. Claim support · 5. Contribution judgment.** Ethics findings, overlap
rejections, withdrawals, and process complaints are outside the funnel.

The agreement pattern across the panel is the primary instrument at every stage.
**Divergent** points to Stage 3, **convergent** to Stage 4, **polarized** to Stage 5. Read
agreement on what is wrong, never agreement on what to do about it.

---

## Stage 1 — Desk rejection

No qualified reader assessed the work. Nothing about its clarity, its evidence, or its
contribution is in evidence, and there is no panel, so there is no comparison set. Most
cases at this stage return Undetermined for anything below Stage 1.

### 1A. Scope visible from the abstract

- **Signature:** the editor's note cites fit. The venue's scope statement excludes the paper's
  topic, method, or population, and that exclusion is legible from the title and abstract
  alone.
- **Rules it out:** the abstract sits squarely inside the published scope.

### 1B. Format or submission non-compliance

- **Signature:** length over limit, missing required sections, anonymisation failure, missing
  statements the venue requires. The note cites the requirement.
- **Rules it out:** submission met the stated requirements.
- **Note:** cheap to check and it ends the process before any reading. Check it before
  anything interpretive.

### 1C. Volume triage

- **Signature:** a generic note, no specific objection, at a venue with a published desk-reject
  rate. Timing days after submission.
- **Rules it out:** an itemised editorial objection.
- **Note:** this is Stage 1's version of the null and it frequently *is* the null. Route to
  Step 5.

### 1D. Overlap or prior publication flag

- **Signature:** the note cites a specific prior work, a preprint, or a similarity report.
- **Rules it out:** no overlap identified.
- **Note:** outside the funnel if the finding concerns research conduct rather than novelty.

## Stage 2 — Break at reviewer engagement

Reports exist and there is not enough in them to read. What is in evidence: whether reviewers
worked through the paper. What is not: anything about the paper itself.

### 2A. Cursory reports

- **Signature:** very short reports, no section named, no claim quoted, no page cited. Generic
  praise or generic dismissal that would fit any paper in the field.
- **Rules it out:** reports quoting the paper and naming specific sections.
- **Note:** a short report is not automatically cursory. A reviewer who names one specific
  fatal objection in four lines has engaged. Read for specificity, not length.

### 2B. Reviewer mismatch

- **Signature:** reports state uncertainty about the subfield, ask questions the paper's
  standard background answers, or assess the paper against the conventions of a neighbouring
  area. No report demonstrates command of the specific literature.
- **Rules it out:** at least one report demonstrating command of the area.

### 2C. Thin panel forced by declines

- **Signature:** the decision rests on fewer reports than the venue normally uses; editor's
  note mentions difficulty securing reviewers or a delayed round.
- **Rules it out:** a full panel returned.
- **Note:** this weakens the panel as an instrument and belongs in the integrity check as
  well as here.

### 2D. Delegation

- **Signature:** a report's voice, depth, or framing differs markedly from what the invitation
  implies; the venue permits sub-reviewers and one report reads as a first pass.
- **Rules it out:** no indication of delegation.
- **Note:** this is an observation about a report, not about a person. Do not speculate about
  who wrote it.

## Stage 3 — Break at reconstruction

The reports are substantive and the readers did not arrive at the paper. **This is where a
divergent pattern lands, and it is the finding the folk cause is designed to hide** — "one
reviewer didn't read it" is a Stage 3 claim made about one reviewer, when the evidence usually
shows all of them arriving somewhere different.

Everything downstream is starved here. An evidential objection written by a reviewer who
restated the claim incorrectly is an objection to a paper that was not submitted.

### 3A. Claim ambiguity

The paper asserts at more than one scope and never marks which is being claimed.

- **Signature:** each report restates the contribution differently, and **the restatements
  conflict with each other**. One reads it as a general method, one as a result on a single
  dataset, one as a framework. Each objection is internally coherent and answers a different
  paper.
- **Rules it out:** restatements that agree with each other.

### 3B. Method opacity

- **Signature:** multiple reports ask what was actually done — which data, which condition,
  which comparison — and the questions are answerable from the paper only by inference.
  Objections are conditional: "if the authors mean X, then."
- **Rules it out:** reports restating the method correctly and objecting to it.

### 3C. Framing mismatch

The paper is filed against the wrong literature, so readers evaluate it by the wrong standard.

- **Signature:** the reports **agree with each other** about what the paper is, and all of them
  differ from the author in the same direction. They cite the same neighbouring literature as
  the relevant baseline and judge against its conventions.
- **Rules it out:** reports locating the paper where the author locates it.
- **Does NOT separate it from 3A:** the number of misreadings, or the count of divergent
  objections. Both 3A and 3C produce reports whose account of the paper differs from the
  author's, and both produce objections that miss. **Agreement among the readers decides, not
  the count of misreadings.** In 3A the restatements conflict with each other: three readers,
  three different papers. In 3C the restatements agree with each other and differ only from
  the author's: three readers, one paper, and it is not the one that was written. A paper that
  reads differently to everyone is ambiguous. A paper that reads the same wrong way to
  everyone communicated successfully, just not the intended thing.

> **General form of that rule.** When two causes in the same stage produce the same count on
> the same measure, that count screens them in together and cannot rank them. Find the
> property that differs between the two and read that instead. A count that both hypotheses
> predict is not a discriminator.

### 3D. Structural burial

The contribution is present, correct, and not locatable.

- **Signature:** reports identify the paper's topic correctly but describe the contribution as
  smaller than it is, or name a secondary result as the main one. The actual claim appears
  late, in a subsection, or only in the discussion.
- **Rules it out:** reports naming the intended contribution as the contribution.

## Stage 4 — Break at claim support

Readers arrived at the paper and did not accept that the evidence supports it. **This is where
a convergent pattern lands**, and convergence from readers who never spoke is the strongest
signal available in this domain.

Before naming any cause here, run the pass-through: count objections from reviewers who
**reconstructed the paper correctly**. Objections from a misreading belong to Stage 3.

### 4A. Evidential gap

- **Signature:** independent reports name the same distance between what was shown and what was
  claimed. Frequently phrased as scope of generalisation.
- **Rules it out:** objections that do not overlap, or that dispute the claim's interest rather
  than its support.

### 4B. Missing comparison, baseline, or ablation

- **Signature:** reports name the same absent comparison. Highly convergent when real, because
  the missing item is obvious to anyone with the relevant training.
- **Rules it out:** each report naming a different absent comparison, which is a Stage 3
  signature — readers reconstructing different claims require different comparisons.

### 4C. Method validity objection

- **Signature:** reports converge on the same step as invalid for the inference drawn.
- **Rules it out:** convergence on the description of the method rather than its validity,
  which is 3B.

### 4D. Analytic or statistical objection

- **Signature:** reports converge on the same analytic decision — the test, the correction, the
  unit, the exclusion rule.
- **Rules it out:** scattered analytic comments of the kind every report contains.

### 4E. Unaddressed alternative explanation

- **Signature:** reports independently propose the same alternative account the paper does not
  rule out. When two readers who never spoke reach for the same confound, that is close to the
  cleanest evidence this domain produces.
- **Rules it out:** different alternatives proposed by each reviewer, which points back to
  ambiguity about what is being claimed.

## Stage 5 — Break at contribution judgment

Readers arrived at the paper, accepted that it does what it says, and judged that it does not
belong here. **This is where a polarized panel lands.** The work is not the constraint; the
venue is.

The signature of the stage as a whole: reports **agree on the facts and split on the verdict's
grounds** — not merely on the recommendation. One report treats the contribution as
sufficient, another treats the same contribution as insufficient, and both describe it
identically.

### 5A. Venue scope mismatch

- **Signature:** at least one report explicitly questions fit while accepting the work.
  Reviewers disagree about which literature the paper should be judged against, and the
  decision letter adopts one of the two readings.
- **Rules it out:** all reports treating the paper as in-scope.
- **Note:** where the scope objection is legible from the abstract alone and the paper still
  went to review, the editorial screen let through something the panel then caught. That is
  still Stage 5.

### 5B. Incrementality against this venue's bar

- **Signature:** reports accept correctness and dispute sufficiency, referencing the venue's
  standard rather than the field's. Language about "expected at this venue."
- **Rules it out:** disputes about correctness rather than sufficiency.

### 5C. Audience mismatch

- **Signature:** reports accept the work and question who at this venue would read it.
- **Rules it out:** reports engaging as the paper's audience.

### 5D. Competitive triage

- **Signature:** reports positive or neutral, no substantive objection convergent or otherwise,
  decision letter citing volume or a cutoff.
- **Rules it out:** any convergent substantive objection.
- **Note:** this is the null wearing a Stage 5 costume, and the distinction is worth
  maintaining. 5D is a finding about a paper that cleared the bar and lost a queue. The null is
  a finding about a decision that carries no signal at all. If the reports contain positive
  assessments of the contribution and the letter cites competition, prefer 5D and say so. If
  the reports contain nothing either way, prefer the null.

---

## Outside the funnel — Not a review failure

- **Ethics, integrity, or research-conduct findings.** A different process with different
  rules and different stakes. This folder does not diagnose it and does not comment on it.
- **Prior publication or overlap.** A publishability question, not a review outcome.
- **Author withdrawal.** No decision to diagnose.
- **Process complaint** — conflict of interest, a reviewer who should have recused, a
  procedural failure. That is an appeal to the editor. Say plainly that the constraint sits
  outside the paper, and stop; do not build a case for the appeal.

---

## The null: ordinary outcome at a high-rejection venue

Nothing about this paper produced this decision.

- **Signature:** the venue rejects most of what it receives. No objection appears in more than
  one report. The objections present are the ordinary furniture of review rather than claims
  the paper fails. The decision letter cites volume, competition, or fit in general terms. No
  reviewer misread the paper and none disputed its scope.
- **Rules it out:** any convergent substantive objection, a demonstrable misreading, or a sharp
  split on the paper's grounds.

A paper that drew three ordinary reports at a venue that rejects 85% of submissions has not
been told anything about itself. Rewriting it addresses nothing, because the decision was not
about it.
