# Intake

What a case needs before a diagnosis is possible, and what to do when it is missing.

---

## Required

Without all four, the funnel cannot be reconstructed and the correct output is
**Undetermined**.

1. **All reviewer reports, in full, as written.** Every report the author received, not a
   selection and not a summary.
2. **The decision letter**, including the editor's own remarks where present. This is what
   tells you which reading the venue acted on.
3. **The submitted version of the paper** — at minimum the title, abstract, claim statements,
   and the sections the reports name. A reviewer's restatement can only be checked against the
   paper.
4. **The venue and its stated scope**, plus its rejection rate where published. The rate is the
   input the null model runs on.

The first is the one authors most often compromise, usually without meaning to.

**A selected panel cannot show divergence.** An author who supplies the two reports that stung
and leaves out the mild one has handed you a set from which only convergence can be read, and
convergence read off a selected panel is an artifact of the selection. Ask explicitly whether
every report received is present. If reports are missing, say so, and work out which direction
the absence pushes before drawing anything.

**Summaries destroy the instrument.** The pattern lives in whether two reports objected to the
same thing, which requires the actual wording of both. An author's summary of a report is a
reading of it, produced by the person whose paper it is.

---

## Strongly wanted

Each of these decides a specific branch. Note in the report which were absent.

- **The venue's review process**, specifically whether reviewers see each other's reports or
  participate in a discussion phase before finalising. This decides whether the panel is
  independent, which is the assumption everything else rests on.
- **Report metadata** — submission dates, version markers, reviewer numbering as assigned by
  the venue.
- **Prior submission history** — earlier rounds at this or another venue, with their reports.
  The input the qualifying revision test runs on. Include **whether the venue, the handling editor,
  and the scope statement were the same across rounds**, and whether the resubmission was routed to
  a fresh panel: the fourth qualifying condition in `rules.md` turns on exactly those, and without
  them a prior round cannot be graded at all.
- **The revision itself**, where this is a resubmission: what changed, section by section.
- **The venue's reviewer instructions or rubric**, which tells you what reviewers were asked to
  weigh and separates an objection they volunteered from one they were prompted for.
- **The editor's assignment note**, where the author has it.

---

## Useful

- The paper's prior public form — preprint, talk, workshop version — and any feedback on it
- Reviewer expertise as declared by the venue, at the level of area rather than identity
- The venue's published acceptance profile: what kind of paper it takes
- Author's own account of what they intended to claim, which is checked against the paper
  rather than accepted

---

## Handling missing evidence

**Never substitute inference for a missing input.** The failure mode this folder exists to
prevent is a confident cause built on absent data, and its close cousin is a pattern read off a
panel that was never independent.

| Missing | Effect | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| Some reports (a selected panel) | Divergence cannot be observed; convergence is an artifact of selection | Say which reports are absent and work out the direction. A convergent finding on a selected panel is capped; a divergent finding survives, since the missing reports could only add divergence. |
| All but one report | No comparison exists | Undetermined. Name it as the single blocking input. |
| The paper itself | Reconstruction cannot be checked | Undetermined for Stage 3, which means the pass-through cannot be run and no Stage 4 finding is safe. Do not accept a reviewer's restatement as the paper. |
| The decision letter | The editor's reading is unknown | Say so. On a polarized panel this is the blocking input, because the finding is which read the venue adopted. |
| The venue's process | Independence cannot be established | Treat the convergent branch as capped and say why. Divergent findings are unaffected. |
| The venue's rejection rate | Null untestable against its own input | State that the null could not be measured, which caps it at Provisional under the null-grading rung, regardless of how ordinary the reports look. |
| Prior round's reports | The revision test cannot run | Report the revision as Uninformative and say the prior pattern is unavailable, rather than assuming it. |

The venue-rate row matters most for nulls, and the selected-panel row matters most for
everything else.

## Reading the reports

The reports are primary evidence. Read them whole, and read them against each other rather
than one at a time — the pattern does not exist inside any single report.

What to extract from each, and only in the branches where it is live:

- **The restatement, for Stage 3.** What does this reviewer say the paper claims and does? Pull
  it out explicitly, in their words, before reading any objection. Then compare restatements to
  each other and to the paper. This is the single most diagnostic extraction in the folder and
  it is routinely skipped, because objections are louder than restatements.
- **The substantive objections, for Stage 4.** The ones bearing on whether the paper should be
  published, separated from requests for another citation or a clearer figure. Then check
  overlap in substance, not in wording.
- **Statements about fit, novelty, and audience, for Stage 5.** Frequently sit in the
  confidential-to-editor section rather than the author-facing one.
- **The recommendation, read separately from all of the above.** It is not the pattern. A panel
  can converge on a defect and split on the verdict, and that is a fact about the reviewers'
  thresholds rather than about the paper.

**Tone carries almost nothing.** A dismissive report and a generous one can contain identical
findings, and the same objection appears as "the authors have not considered" and as "it would
strengthen the paper to consider." Strip the register and read the claim underneath. Authors
read tone first, which is exactly why a diagnosis that reads tone adds nothing they did not
already have.

**Length is not engagement.** A reviewer who names one specific fatal objection in four lines
has worked through the paper. A reviewer who produces three paragraphs of field-generic
commentary has not. Read for whether the paper is quoted, sections are named, and claims are
addressed as stated.

---

## What is not read, and why

**The science is not assessed.** This folder does not evaluate whether the claims are true,
whether the method is sound, whether the analysis is right, or whether any objection is
correct. It cannot tell an author that reviewer 2 was wrong. That question is answered by the
field, over time, and a tool that answered it from three reports would be producing confident
noise about the thing that matters most.

What it does instead is treat the reports as **evidence about how the paper was received**.
That is a question the reports can actually answer, because they are the complete record of it.

**Reviewers are not identified or characterized.** No inference about who wrote a report, their
seniority, their institution, their field beyond what the venue declared, their motives, their
competence, or their good faith. Reviewers are numbered as the venue numbered them. Anonymity
is a condition of the process working at all, and a tool that erodes it in a public repository
would be doing damage well beyond one paper.

Where a report is thin or appears delegated, that is recorded as an observation about the
report — its specificity, its engagement with the text — never as a judgment about a person.

---

## Privacy

Author names, institutional affiliations, reviewer identifiers beyond the venue's numbering,
and editor names are not needed and should not appear in the report. Refer to parties by role
and number. Report text is quoted for its content and attributed to nobody beyond "Reviewer 2."

Reports are confidential documents in most venues' processes. Whether to place them in a
project is the author's disclosure decision and not this folder's. Either way the report does
not reproduce them beyond the phrases the argument requires.
